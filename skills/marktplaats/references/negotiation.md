# Replying and closing

## Voice

Mirror the buyer's language — Dutch if they write Dutch, English if English. Informal `je`, never
`u`. Short. Answer the question they asked and stop.

Marktplaats messages are two or three lines. A paragraph reads like a sales pitch and kills
momentum. No greetings-plus-signature ceremony, no "Hopelijk hoor ik snel van je!" filler.

Marktplaats' own community guidance, worth following because buyers expect it: always reply, even
to say no; be clear; keep the appointments you make.

## Listing copy — write what the market writes

Before drafting, **read three or four live comparables in full** and match their register. Observed
across 20+ KitchenAid listings, 2026-08-16:

- **Short.** 40-60 words, four or five plain lines. A long ad reads like a shop, not a neighbour.
- **No bullet lists, no headers, no emoji.** Private sellers write sentences. A bulleted spec sheet
  is an immediate tell that the ad was not written by a person.
- **Don't repeat the Kenmerken.** Marktplaats renders Conditie / Inhoud / Snelheden / properties as
  a structured block *above* the description. Restating them in prose wastes the reader's attention
  on something the page already shows.
- **State the reason for sale.** It is a genuine convention and it answers the unspoken "what's
  wrong with it?" — *"Wordt verkocht vanwege aanschaf alles-in-één apparaat."*
- **Faults go in plainly and early**, in the seller's own words — *"Enkel iets verkleurd."*
- **Retail price: usually omit.** Only 1 of 14 listings quoted one. It invites the buyer to go
  price-check, which can surface a promo that undercuts the asking price. Use it only when the item
  is genuinely near-new and the gap to retail is the main selling point.
- **Model numbers in titles are normal** and help search — *"KitchenAid Foodprocessor 1.7L
  Keizerrood (5KFP0719EER)"*. Use the Dutch colour name the retailers use (`Keizerrood`, not
  `Empire Red`).

## Reading traction on a comparable

A listing detail page shows **`<n>x bekeken`**, **`<n>x bewaard`** and **`Sinds <date>`** near the
seller block. This is the cheapest read available on whether a price works — and it works on other
people's listings, not just the user's. A comparable with plenty of views, days on site and no sale
means that price is not clearing, whatever the seller is asking. Quote these numbers when proposing
a price; they are evidence, not decoration.

## The five questions, and how to pre-empt them

Nearly every inquiry is one of:

1. *"Is het nog beschikbaar?"* — Yes/no, plus one line moving it forward.
2. *"Wat is de laatste prijs?"* — See below.
3. *"Kun je verzenden?"* — Answer from the item's delivery setting.
4. *"Kan ik hem komen bekijken?"* — Offer two concrete time windows, not "wanneer schikt het?".
5. *"Werkt alles / zijn er gebreken?"* — Answer honestly from `brief.md`. Never soften a known fault.

A good listing description answers all five, which is why fewer messages is a sign the listing is
working.

## Price talk

- Hold the asking price on the first ask. A quick discount signals the price was padded and
  invites a second round.
- Never go below the floor in `brief.md` without asking the user first.
- **The floor is internal. It is never disclosed to a buyer** — not stated, not hinted at, not
  used as a justification ("ik kan tot €X gaan", "mijn ondergrens is…"), and not implied by
  volunteering a discount nobody asked for. It silently decides whether an offer is acceptable,
  and nothing more. A buyer who learns the floor will offer exactly it, so revealing it converts
  the whole gap between asking and floor into a guaranteed loss.
- Counter with a number *above* the floor and let the floor stay invisible. A counter that lands
  exactly on it leaves nowhere to go in the next round.
- Trading down is fine when it closes: "€X als je hem deze week ophaalt."
- If a buyer bids through the **Biedingen** flow rather than messaging, treat it as an offer with
  the same rules — it is a separate surface but the same decision.
- Multiple interested buyers is worth saying plainly and truthfully. Never invent a rival buyer.

## Closing checklist

In order. Each step is a separate message, and each one is approval-gated like any other.

1. **Price agreed** — restate it in writing so both sides have it in the thread.
2. **Delivery agreed** — pickup or shipping.
3. **Payment** — Marktplaats' own payment flow first, Tikkie second. Confirm money received
   *before* handing over or shipping. Cash only if the buyer insists and the user approves.
4. **Time** — a concrete day and time window, not "morgen".
5. **Address** — only now, and only via the **`Deel adres`** button. Never typed into a message.
6. **Mark `reserved`** in `ledger.md` and `brief.md`, with who and when.
7. **After handover** — mark `sold`, close the listing, and tell the other interested buyers the
   item is gone. That last step is what earns the review.

If a buyer goes quiet after agreeing, one polite nudge, then release the reservation and tell them.

## Red flags — flag, never play along

These get surfaced in the report and never receive a drafted reply that engages with the premise.

- **A payment request "to verify your account"** — any €0,01 / €1,00 Tikkie or betaalverzoek sent
  *to the seller*. This is always a scam. Sellers never pay to receive money.
- **A link to a "Marktplaats payment" page sent in chat.** The real flow lives inside Marktplaats'
  own interface, not behind a link a buyer pastes.
- **Pressure to move to WhatsApp, email or SMS** early, especially before price is agreed. Getting
  off-platform is the point for a scammer — it removes the paper trail and the protections.
- **Overpayment then a refund request.** Always fraud.
- **"My courier/transporter will collect and pay"** — a courier that pays on the seller's behalf
  does not exist.
- **A buyer asking for bank details, DigiD, or an ID scan.** None of these are needed to sell a
  second-hand item.
- **An immediate full-price yes, sight unseen, on a high-value item**, paired with urgency.

None of these need a clever reply. Report them to the user, and if they want a response the right
one is short and refuses the premise.
