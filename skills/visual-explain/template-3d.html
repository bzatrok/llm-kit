Four-stroke engine, in 3D

Same story as the flat version. The crank angle drives every part: piston height, conrod swing, valve lift, charge colour. Each step only says "go to this angle, look from here, highlight this part".

The parts. Cylinder, piston, connecting rod, crank. Two valves in the head, spark plug between them. The rod is what converts straight travel into rotation, and it swings as it does so.

Intake, 0° to 180°. Piston descends, the intake valve lifts, the chamber fills with a blue air and fuel charge. The engine spends energy here, from the flywheel.

Compression, 180° to 360°. Valves closed, piston rises, the charge turns dark as it is squeezed to about a tenth of its volume.

Ignition, around 350°. The plug fires just before top dead centre. The flash is brief. From above you can see why the plug sits centrally: the flame must reach every wall.

Power, 360° to 540°. Hot gas expands and drives the piston down. The rod is angled now, so the push becomes torque. Only this stroke does work.

Exhaust, 540° to 720°. Exhaust valve lifts, the piston sweeps the grey burnt gas out. Back at top dead centre, the cycle starts again.

At speed. Two full turns per power stroke. Here it runs continuously. Watch how little of the time the gas is orange: that is why single cylinders shake and why engines add cylinders out of phase.
