Mild hybrid or normal petrolTo Sam · 4 Mar
The mild hybrid has a small extra battery and a small electric motor. It's not a "real" hybrid: it can't drive on electricity. It only helps the petrol engine a little when you pull away, and lets the engine switch off for a moment when you take your foot off the gas. Driving: you won't notice a difference. Same power, same speed, same 0–100 time. Fuel: the hybrid saves very little, about 12 litres a year for normal driving. Downsides of the hybrid: - The battery sits under the boot floor, so the boot is about a quarter smaller (255 litres instead of 340). - More parts that can break once the car is out of warranty: the battery, the electric motor and the clutch, which is worked by electronics on the hybrid. Conclusion: the hybrid isn't worth choosing a car for. If boot space matters to us, normal petrol is better.
Dealer questionsFor any new listing
1. Can you send the full service history (Kestrel service passport or invoices) and the mileage-check report? 2. What is the VIN? 3. How many owners, and was the car ever a rental or lease car? 4. Does the price include a new inspection, a service and trade-association warranty? What is the price with that warranty? 5. When were the brakes and the timing chain last done?
